Advancing the understanding of bicycle dynamics under real-world conditions 
requires not only reliable measurement tools but also accessible and 
reproducible research practices. Despite growing interest in this field, 
existing experimental setups often rely on proprietary or custom-built systems 
that are difficult to replicate. This lack of standardisation limits 
comparability across studies and highlights the need for an open research 
infrastructure.

In this project, we adopt an open science approach to the development of a 
bicycle data logger, with a focus on modularity and community-driven 
development. The complete project, including hardware designs, software, 
documentation, and research outputs, is maintained in a public GitHub repository 
under the MIT license. The repository consolidates technical documentation, 
source code, design files, and research outputs, ensuring that the entire 
development process follows open-source principles.

To promote reproducibility and long-term usability, the system is built 
exclusively from off-the-shelf, easily replaceable components. This approach 
enables adaptation to diverse experimental requirements while facilitating 
maintenance and future updates. The project has been supported by the TU Delft 
Open Science initiative, reflecting its alignment with institutional efforts to 
promote transparent and reusable research.

A key challenge in open hardware projects is encouraging community adoption and 
contribution. To address this, we prioritise usability and low barriers to entry, 
aiming to make the platform easier to deploy than developing a new system from 
scratch. This is supported by a plug-and-play design philosophy, complemented by 
detailed documentation, step-by-step instructions, and troubleshooting guides. 
Collaboration is facilitated through open development practices, including issue 
tracking and community contributions.

By combining open hardware, documentation, and collaboration, this work 
contributes to a more reproducible and scalable research ecosystem for bicycle 
dynamics research. Future work will focus on expanding the range of ready-to-use 
sensor modules and strengthening community engagement.
